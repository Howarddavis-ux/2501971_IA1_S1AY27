The Richest Man in Babylon

Click to Read

This book has presented me with a plethora of remedies to alleviate my financial constraints. Out of the seven cures, there were two that really resonated with me are cure 1 and 2, highlighting the importance of shifting one's mind from passively spending to strategically allocating funds. Someone can start with diverting a portion of their income stream, into savings or as Arkhad would say “into one's purse” but without proper control as quickly as the purse fattens it can also lose all its content. Proper control is essential, as it ensures that your purse grows passively while allocating expenses towards items that are necessary for survival.

Furthermore, of the five laws of gold, the ones that deeply resonate with the current stage of life are the third and fifth laws. As I am entering the adult world, there are a lot of things I am unfamiliar with, one of which is finance. Being able to connect with a mentor or a guiding figure who has spent years making mistakes and learning from them. Absorbing their knowledge, avoid making reckless decisions and start transitioning into more cautious decision-making as gold clingeth to the cautious owner.

Equally important is the fifth law, which wards against the illusive nature of easy wealth. It is easy to be misled by individuals who are looking to make money and have no intention to facilitate their mentees. Industries such as the Forex market are filled with individuals selling a false dream that someone can make an unfathomable amount of money in a week, if they were to purchase their course. Resulting in many people at the end of the course realizing that life they were told was false.

My biggest take away from this book is that a person's success isn’t always tied to their profession but the mentality they have towards managing their “stream of gold”. If Arkhad, who was only a scribe managed to achieve great riches, just means that everyone also can.
